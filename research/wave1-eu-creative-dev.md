# Wave 1 — European creative-dev techniques for a scroll-morphing particle site

Target: Three.js site, ~28,000 GPU particles morphing sphere → funnel → DNA helix → wave horizon → galaxy on scroll, with bloom, depth of field, line structures, dark background, large typography.

Date of research: 2026-10-06. Method: public pages only (WebSearch + WebFetch). Paid lesson bodies were not read.

Rule format: **Technique** — formula / values / snippet — when to use — source + URL — tag.
Tags: `[verified]` = read on a fetched page during this research. `[recalled]` = general knowledge, not confirmed on a fetched page (includes content I know from paid lessons or docs that were not reachable). ## 1. Particle rendering quality

1. **Perspective size attenuation in a custom shader** — `gl_PointSize = size * (1.0 / -viewPosition.z);` — mandatory as soon as `PointsMaterial` is replaced by `ShaderMaterial`; without it all particles are the same pixel size and depth is lost. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/the-magical-world-of-particles-with-react-three-fiber-and-shaders/ `[verified]`
2. **Per-particle scale in the attenuation** — `gl_PointSize = uSize * aScale * (1.0 / -viewPosition.z);` with `aScale` a random attribute — gives size variety so the cloud reads as matter, not a grid of equal dots. — Codrops / UntilLabs, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/12/10/simulating-life-in-the-browser-creating-a-living-particle-system-for-the-untillabs-website/ `[verified]`
3. **Resolution- and DPR-independent size** — `gl_PointSize = aSize * uSize * uResolution.y; gl_PointSize *= (1.0 / -viewPosition.z);` where `uResolution = (width*dpr, height*dpr)` — keeps the composition identical on a phone and a 4K display. The free part of the lesson only states the problem ("1 fragment is smaller than 1 pixel on retina"); the fix is in the paid part. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/animated-galaxy `[recalled]`
4. **Soft disc with power falloff** — `float d = length(gl_PointCoord - 0.5); float a = pow(1.0 - smoothstep(0.0, 0.5, d), 1.5);` — default particle sprite: no hard edge, cheap, bloom-friendly. — Codrops / UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]`
5. **Glow point (cubic falloff)** — `float s = 1.0 - distance(gl_PointCoord, vec2(0.5)); s = pow(s, 3.0);` then `mix()` colours by `s` — hotter core, wide dim halo. — Maxime Heckel (URL in 1) `[verified]`
6. **"Light point" by small-number division** — `float alpha = 0.05 / distanceToCenter;` (in the paid part an offset `- 0.1` is subtracted so alpha reaches 0 before the quad edge) — very bright pin-point core with fast fade; the best base for additive galaxies. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/particles-morphing-shader `[verified]` (the `- 0.1` offset is recalled)
7. **Alpha must hit zero before the sprite edge** — any falloff that is not zero at `d = 0.5` leaves a faint square that bloom then amplifies; clamp with `max(0.0, …)` or subtract an offset. — general `[recalled]`
8. **Points = one draw call** — 60,000 `GL_POINTS` in a single draw call at a constant 60 fps; "each particle is a plane always facing the camera", hundreds of thousands are feasible. — UntilLabs (URL in 2); Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/particles `[verified]`
9. **Instanced quads instead of points** — one quad geometry + `InstancedBufferGeometry`, shared data in `BufferAttribute`, per-instance data in `InstancedBufferAttribute` — use when sprites must be rotated, stretched along velocity, non-square, or larger than the driver's max point size. — Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/01/17/interactive-particles-with-three-js/ `[verified]`
10. **Point size ceiling** — `gl.getParameter(gl.ALIASED_POINT_SIZE_RANGE)` caps `gl_PointSize` (often 64–1024 px depending on GPU); large out-of-focus bokeh discs on mobile can be silently clamped → switch those to instanced quads. — general `[recalled]`
11. **Additive material baseline** — `depthWrite: false, blending: THREE.AdditiveBlending, sizeAttenuation: true` — standard for luminous particles on dark backgrounds; no sorting needed. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/galaxy-generator `[verified]`
12. **Additive saturation pitfall** — overlapping particles sum to white and kill colour; observed in Codrops demos where four coloured boxes blend to white. Fix: lower per-particle alpha as density rises, and render into an HDR buffer so tone mapping (not clipping) handles the overlap. — Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2017/12/12/3d-particle-explorations/ `[verified]` (the fix is recalled)
13. **Global exposure uniform for particles** — keep one `uExposure` multiplier on final particle colour; Blurry ships `exposure: 0.009` (and `0.0019` in the README scene) because tens of thousands of additive samples accumulate. — Codrops / Blurry, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/10/01/simulating-depth-of-field-with-particles-using-the-blurry-library/ and https://github.com/Domenicobrz/Blurry `[verified]`
14. **HDR render target** — `new EffectComposer(renderer, { frameBufferType: HalfFloatType })`; "linear, high precision HalfFloatType buffers" remove banding in dark scenes. UntilLabs renders its particle scene to a 16-bit half-float FBO "for HDR headroom". — pmndrs postprocessing, https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing ; UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]`
15. **Tone mapping belongs at the end of the chain** — set `renderer.toneMapping = NoToneMapping`, add `ToneMappingEffect` last. Per-material `toneMapped` "only works when rendering directly to the screen". — pmndrs README (URL in 14); three.js forum, https://discourse.threejs.org/t/pmndrs-post-processing-tone-mapping-guidance/59374 `[verified]`
16. **ACES vs AgX** — ACES Filmic "shifts the hue more than you might want"; AgX (from Blender 4) is the cleaner alternative. For a brand-coloured particle cloud, AgX or Neutral keeps hue; ACES gives the contrasty "film" look. — three.js forum (URL in 15) `[verified]` (`THREE.AgXToneMapping` / `NeutralToneMapping` constants are recalled)
17. **Use tone-mapping desaturation deliberately** — colours above 1.0 desaturate toward white under tone mapping while surroundings keep saturation; so push dense cores above 1.0 and they read as white-hot with coloured halos. — three.js forum (URL in 15) `[verified]`
18. **ACES fit in one line** — `c = clamp((x*(2.51*x+0.03))/(x*(2.43*x+0.59)+0.14), 0.0, 1.0);` — when tone mapping inside your own final shader. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/on-crafting-painterly-shaders/ `[verified]`
19. **Exposure as a tuned parameter** — `renderer.toneMappingExposure`, tweak in a GUI over 0–10 with 0.001 steps; tone mapping options listed: No, Linear, Reinhard, Cineon, ACESFilmic. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/realistic-render `[verified]`
20. **Cosine palette** — `vec3 palette(float t, vec3 a, vec3 b, vec3 c, vec3 d){ return a + b*cos(6.28318*(c*t+d)); }` e.g. `a=b=(.5,.5,.5), c=(1,1,1), d=(0,.33,.67)` (rainbow) or `d=(0,.10,.20)` (cool monochrome drift), `c=(1,.7,.4), d=(0,.15,.20)` (warm) — colour particles by radius, noise or progress with 12 floats instead of a texture. — Inigo Quilez, https://iquilezles.org/articles/palettes/ `[verified]`
21. **Colour by displacement distance** — `vec3 color = mix(startColor, endColor, vDistance); gl_FragColor = vec4(color, circ.r * vDistance);` — particles that moved furthest from rest get the accent colour and more alpha. — Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2023/12/19/creating-audio-reactive-visuals-with-dynamic-particles-in-three-js/ `[verified]`
22. **Saturation control** — `vec3 g = vec3(dot(rgb, vec3(0.2125,0.7154,0.0721))); rgb = mix(g, rgb, 1.5);` — final grade knob (1.0 = neutral). — Maxime Heckel (URL in 18) `[verified]`
23. **LUT colour grading as last fullscreen pass** — designer-swappable lookup texture applied in the final quad shader, no code change per look. — UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]`
24. **Dither to kill banding in dark gradients** — hash: `fract(sin(dot(c, vec2(12.9898,78.233))) * 43758.5453)`; add `(hash - 0.5) / 255.0` to the final colour after tone mapping. Ordered alternative: Bayer 4×4 `1/16·[0,8,2,10; 12,4,14,6; 3,11,1,9; 15,7,13,5]`; best quality: blue-noise texture. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/the-art-of-dithering-and-retro-shading-web/ `[verified]` (the `±0.5/255` application is recalled)
25. **Temporal blue-noise offset** — `fract(blueNoise + float(frame % 32) / sqrt(0.5))` — rotates the dither pattern each frame so it averages out. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/shaping-light-volumetric-lighting-with-post-processing-and-raymarching/ `[verified]`
26. **sRGB output** — `renderer.outputColorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace` (replaces `outputEncoding` since r152); do all additive maths in linear, convert once at the end. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/post-processing ; pmndrs README `[verified]`
27. **Size from data** — particle size driven by noise combined with source brightness — instantly breaks uniformity. — Codrops (URL in 9) `[verified]`
28. **Drop invisible particles at build time** — discard source pixels darker than `#22` before creating the buffer — fewer vertices, same picture. — Codrops (URL in 9) `[verified]`
29. **Texture-encoded point clouds** — positions split into hi/lo byte RGB textures (256×256 = 65,536 points), reconstruct `hi*256 + lo`, remap to world bounds; 20 MB JSON → 604 KB. — UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]`
30. **Twinkle** — `size *= 0.75 + 0.25 * sin(uTime * f + aRandom * 6.2831);` — cheap life for galaxy and horizon states. — general `[recalled]`

## 2. Morphing between shapes

31. **Attribute mix (the baseline)** — attributes `position` + `aPositionTarget`, uniform `uProgress` 0→1, `vec3 p = mix(position, aPositionTarget, uProgress);` — done in the vertex shader "rather than CPU updates or GPGPU" for performance. Right tool for 28k particles and deterministic shape-to-shape morphs. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/particles-morphing-shader `[verified]`
32. **Noise-staggered morph (per-particle delay)** —
    ```glsl
    float n = mix(snoise(position*0.2), snoise(aPositionTarget*0.2), uProgress);
    n = smoothstep(-1.0, 1.0, n);
    float duration = 0.4;
    float delay = (1.0 - duration) * n;
    float progress = smoothstep(delay, delay + duration, uProgress);
    vec3 p = mix(position, aPositionTarget, progress);
    ```
    — neighbouring particles leave together, so the shape dissolves in organic patches instead of a linear cross-fade. Highest-value single change for a morph. — Three.js Journey (paid part of lesson in 31) `[recalled]`
33. **Directional stagger** — replace noise in 32 by a spatial term (`position.y`, radius, or angle) → the morph sweeps like a wave or unwinds; mix `0.7*spatial + 0.3*noise` to keep it organic. Same idea as GSAP `stagger: {from: "center" | "edges" | "random"}`. — general; GSAP analogue from Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/09/03/7-must-know-gsap-animation-tips-for-creative-developers/ `[recalled]`
34. **Equalise vertex counts** — pad every shape to the largest count; fill extra slots with randomly re-used points of that shape so no particle is born or dies. — Three.js Journey (paid part) `[recalled]`
35. **N shapes on one scroll** — `float s = uScroll * 4.0; float i = floor(s); float t = fract(s);` choose pair (i, i+1). With 5 shapes use 5 `vec3` attributes (well under the 16-attribute limit) or one data texture row per shape; avoid re-uploading buffers at boundaries. — general `[recalled]`
36. **Scroll replaces time** — "instead of allowing the render loop to increment a time uniform you update it with the progress of a scrollable element"; add damping and handle touch. — three.js forum, https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-control-a-particle-morph-custom-shader-with-mouse-scroll-instead-of-automatic-animation/86338 `[verified]`
37. **One progress uniform, stateless shader, GSAP owns the curve** — `gsap.to(obj, {progress: 1, duration: 0.6, ease: "power2.inOut", onUpdate(){ u.progress.value = obj.progress }})` — easing and sequencing change in JS without touching GLSL. — Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/06/from-shader-uniforms-to-clip-path-wipes-how-gsap-drives-my-portfolio/ `[verified]`
38. **Mid-transition turbulence with a parabola envelope** — `float k = pow(4.0*t*(1.0-t), 2.0); p += curlNoise(p*freq + uTime*0.1) * amp * k;` — zero at both shapes, maximum halfway, so shapes stay crisp at rest and "explode" between. The envelope is IQ's `parabola(x,k) = pow(4x(1-x), k)`; Codrops uses `parabola(progress, 2.)` to peak displacement and chromatic aberration at 50%. — IQ, https://iquilezles.org/articles/functions/ ; Codrops (URL in 37) `[verified]`
39. **Curl target mix** — `vec3 target = position + normal*0.1 + curl(pos*frequency)*amplitude; float d = length(pos - target)/maxDistance; pos = mix(position, target, pow(d, 4.0));` and `gl_PointSize = size + pow(d,3.0)*offsetSize*(1.0/-mvPosition.z);` — displaced particles also grow. — Codrops (URL in 21) `[verified]`
40. **Easing the morph progress** — IQ `gain(x,k)`: `a = 0.5*pow(2.0*((x<0.5)?x:1.0-x), k); return (x<0.5)?a:1.0-a;` (k>1 = sharper S-curve). Quintic smootherstep `f*f*f*(f*(f*6.0-15.0)+10.0)` for zero acceleration at the ends. — IQ (URL in 38) and https://iquilezles.org/articles/gradientnoise/ `[verified]`
41. **Hold zones** — remap each segment so shapes rest: `t = smoothstep(0.2, 0.8, fract(s))` → 20% of each section the shape is fully formed while text is read. — general `[recalled]`
42. **FBO morph** — two DataTextures (shape A, shape B), simulation shader `vec3 pos = mix(origin, destination, timer);` — same result as 31 but positions live in a texture, which lets later passes add persistent forces. — Nicolas Barradeau, https://barradeau.com/blog/?p=621 ; Maxime Heckel (URL in 1) `[verified]`
43. **FBO / GPGPU setup** — positions in a `DataTexture` (`RGBAFormat`, `FloatType`), simulate into a `WebGLRenderTarget` with `NearestFilter`, read that texture as a uniform in the points vertex shader via a per-particle UV. Requires float textures and vertex texture fetch. — Maxime Heckel (URL in 1); Barradeau (URL in 42) `[verified]`
44. **GPGPU flow field** — RGB = XYZ, alpha = extra data (life); positions persist frame to frame in FBO textures; a flow field is "spatialized streams" — a direction per point in space. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/gpgpu-flow-field-particles-shaders `[verified]`
45. **Flow-field update with life cycle** — `particle.a += dt*0.3; if(particle.a >= 1.0){ particle.xyz = base.xyz; particle.a = mod(particle.a,1.0);} else { vec3 flow = vec3(snoise4(vec4(p*freq+0.0,t)), snoise4(vec4(p*freq+1.0,t)), snoise4(vec4(p*freq+2.0,t))); particle.xyz += normalize(flow)*dt*strength; }`; fade size with `smoothstep(0.,.1,life)*smoothstep(1.,.7,life)` — particles peel off the shape and respawn. — Three.js Journey (paid part of 44) `[recalled]`
46. **Flow-field parameter starting points** — frequency `0.207`, strength `3.043`, influence `0.5`, particle size `0.489` (+0.5 for trail heads). — Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/05/05/matrix-sentinels-building-dynamic-particle-trails-with-tsl/ `[verified]`
47. **When GPGPU is worth it** — only if particles need memory (velocity, accumulated drift, trails, collisions, mouse wake that lingers). For shape A→B on scroll at 28k points, attribute mix + stateless noise is cheaper, deterministic and scrubs backwards perfectly; a stateful sim does not scrub. — synthesis of 31, 42–44 `[recalled]`
48. **Stateless "alive" offset (no FBO)** — add `curl(p*f + uTime*speed) * amp` after the mix; because it is a pure function of position and time it survives scrubbing. — general `[recalled]`
49. **Trails via history buffer** — store head positions per frame (`story_count = 5`, `tails_count = 7`), tail segments read older entries — ribbons without geometry rebuilds. — Codrops (URL in 46) `[verified]`
50. **WebGPU/TSL path** — `instancedArray(COUNT,'vec3')` + compute node replaces FBO ping-pong; render with `SpriteNodeMaterial`, additive, `depthWrite:false`. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/field-guide-to-tsl-and-webgpu/ `[verified]`
51. **Attractor as a shape state** — Thomas attractor step `dx = (-b*x + sin(y))*dt`, `b = 0.19`, `dt = 0.015` — a ready "chaos" state between designed shapes. — Maxime Heckel (URL in 50) `[verified]`
52. **Shape points from a mesh** — `const sampler = new MeshSurfaceSampler(mesh).build(); sampler.sample(tmp);` in a loop (15,000 in the tutorial); supports per-face weights, normals and colours. — Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2021/08/31/surface-sampling-in-three-js/ `[verified]`
53. **Uniform sphere volume** — proper spherical coordinates: `theta = 2π·u, phi = acos(2v-1), r = R·cbrt(w)` — no clumping at poles or centre. — UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]` (the exact formula is recalled; the page states "proper spherical coordinates")
54. **Galaxy generator** — `branchAngle = (i % branches)/branches * 2π; spinAngle = radius * spin; rand = pow(random(), randomnessPower) * (random()<0.5?1:-1) * randomness * radius; x = cos(branchAngle+spinAngle)*radius + randX;` colour = `insideColor.lerp(outsideColor, radius/R)`. The public part shows only `count: 1000, size: 0.02` and the additive material. — Three.js Journey (URL in 11) `[recalled]`
55. **Galaxy spin in the vertex shader** — `float a = atan(p.x,p.z); float d = length(p.xz); a += (1.0/d) * uTime * 0.2; p.x = cos(a)*d; p.z = sin(a)*d;` — inner stars rotate faster. — Three.js Journey, animated-galaxy (paid part) `[recalled]`
56. **Phased animation with remap + clamp** — five overlapping phases (expand fast, scale up, fall, scale down, twinkle) each `clamp(remap(uProgress, a, b, 0, 1), 0, 1)` in the vertex shader. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/fireworks-shaders `[verified]` (phases are public; the remap code is recalled)
57. **Cursor displacement through a canvas texture** — draw the cursor trail to an off-screen 2D canvas, sample it in the vertex shader, `displaced.z += t * 20.0 * rndz;` — interaction cost independent of particle count. — Codrops (URL in 9); same principle in Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/particles-cursor-animation-shader `[verified]`
58. **Awwwards course scope (particles from models)** — "Impress everyone with a 3D particle scene" (Fabio Ottaviani, 2.5 h): Blender clean-up → load models → particles → GLSL colour (27 min) → animating particles (24 min) → final touches. Confirms the industry-standard pipeline: bad model → sampled points → shader colour → shader motion. — https://www.awwwards.com/academy/course/impress-everyone-with-a-3d-particle-scene-starting-from-bad-models `[verified]`

## 3. Noise — compact implementations and "alive" motion

59. **fBm (IQ)** —
    ```glsl
    float fbm(vec3 x, float H){ float G=exp2(-H), f=1., a=1., t=0.;
      for(int i=0;i<OCT;i++){ t+=a*noise(f*x); f*=2.; a*=G; } return t; }
    ```
    `G = 0.5` (H = 1) is the standard. — https://iquilezles.org/articles/fbm/ `[verified]`
60. **Detune lacunarity** — use `2.01`, `1.99` instead of `2.0` so octaves do not line up into visible patterns. — IQ (URL in 59) `[verified]`
61. **Rotate between octaves** — apply a rotation matrix to the domain each octave to reduce axis-aligned bias; up to 6 octaves, amplitude halving. — UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]`
62. **Turbulence / ridge** — turbulence: `value += amp * abs(snoise(st))`; ridge: `n = abs(n); n = offset - n; n = n*n;` — sharp creases for the wave-horizon state. — The Book of Shaders, https://thebookofshaders.com/13/ `[verified]`
63. **Domain warping** — `q = vec2(fbm(p), fbm(p+vec2(5.2,1.3))); r = vec2(fbm(p+4.0*q+vec2(1.7,9.2)), fbm(p+4.0*q+vec2(8.3,2.8))); return fbm(p+4.0*r);` colour by `length(q)` and `r.y`. — IQ, https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp/ `[verified]`
64. **3D curl noise (compact)** —
    ```glsl
    vec3 snoiseVec3(vec3 x){ return vec3(snoise(x),
      snoise(vec3(x.y-19.1,x.z+33.4,x.x+47.2)),
      snoise(vec3(x.z+74.2,x.x-124.5,x.y+99.4))); }
    vec3 curlNoise(vec3 p){ const float e=.1; vec3 dx=vec3(e,0,0),dy=vec3(0,e,0),dz=vec3(0,0,e);
      vec3 x0=snoiseVec3(p-dx),x1=snoiseVec3(p+dx),y0=snoiseVec3(p-dy),y1=snoiseVec3(p+dy),z0=snoiseVec3(p-dz),z1=snoiseVec3(p+dz);
      return normalize(vec3(y1.z-y0.z-z1.y+z0.y, z1.x-z0.x-x1.z+x0.z, x1.y-x0.y-y1.x+y0.x)/(2.*e)); }
    ```
    — divergence-free: particles swirl without clumping or thinning. — cabbibo, https://github.com/cabbibo/glsl-curl-noise/blob/master/curl.glsl `[verified]`
65. **Curl cost** — the version in 64 is 6 × 3 = 18 simplex evaluations per vertex. Fine at 28k vertices on desktop; on mobile halve it by evaluating only during transitions (multiply by the envelope in 38 and skip when it is 0) or use a cheaper divergence-free noise (bitangent noise). — derived from 64; https://github.com/atyuwen/bitangent_noise `[recalled]`
66. **2D curl from fBm** — sample fBm at `±0.01`, finite differences, return `vec2(dy, -dx)` — enough for the flat wave-horizon state. — UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]`
67. **lygia one-liners** — `#include "lygia/generative/curl.glsl"` (also `snoise`, `fbm`, `cnoise`, `voronoi`; `color/tonemap`, `palette`, `dither`; `animation/easing`; `sdf`); `#define CURL_UNNORMALIZED` keeps magnitude variation (natural gusts) instead of constant speed. Install via npm + vite GLSL plugin, or the `https://lygia.xyz/resolve.js` resolver. — https://lygia.xyz/ and https://lygia.xyz/generative/curl `[verified]`
68. **Simplex source** — Ashima/stegu `webgl-noise` `snoise(vec3)` / `snoise(vec4)` is the usual paste-in; 4D (xyz + time) lets the field evolve in place instead of sliding. — general `[recalled]`
69. **Quintic interpolant** — `u = f*f*f*(f*(f*6.-15.)+10.)`, derivative `du = 30.*f*f*(f*(f-2.)+1.)` — analytical derivatives give gradients without extra samples. — IQ (URL in 40) `[verified]`
70. **Hash random** — `fract(sin(dot(c, vec2(12.9898,78.233))) * 43758.5453)` — per-particle seeds in the shader when you do not want another attribute. — Maxime Heckel (URL in 24) `[verified]`
71. **Idle breathing without noise** — per-particle sin/cos oscillation on x, y, z scaled by a random vec3 attribute, amplitudes ≈ 0.3, 0.3, 0.2 — nearly free, keeps resting shapes alive. — UntilLabs (URL in 2) `[verified]`
72. **Shaping toolbox (IQ)** — `expImpulse(x,k) = k*x*exp(1.-k*x)` (attack/decay bursts on section change); `cubicPulse(c,w,x)` (local band, e.g. a highlight ring sweeping the shape); `pcurve(x,a,b)` (asymmetric bump); `almostIdentity(x,n) = sqrt(x*x+n)` (soft abs); `expStep(x,k,n) = exp(-k*pow(x,n))`. — https://iquilezles.org/articles/functions/ `[verified]`
73. **Band with two smoothsteps** — `smoothstep(.2,.5,x) - smoothstep(.5,.8,x)` — select a slice of particles by height or radius for a travelling pulse. — The Book of Shaders, https://thebookofshaders.com/05/ `[verified]`
74. **Wave horizon vertex formula** — `float wave = uAmplitude * sin(position.x * uWaveLength + uTime); p.z += wave;` time step 0.04/frame, amplitude 0–2, wavelength 0–20; amplitude can be mapped to scroll `[amplitude → 0]`. Layer two or three with different directions plus fBm for a sea-like horizon. — Olivier Larose, https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/3d-wave-on-scroll `[verified]`

## 4. Depth — DOF, fog, camera, damping

75. **Per-particle depth of field (the right DOF for additive points)** — in the vertex shader: `float coc = abs(-viewPosition.z - uFocus) * uAperture; gl_PointSize = base + coc * uResolution.y;` and conserve energy in alpha: `vAlpha = base*base / (gl_PointSize*gl_PointSize);` — big, faint discs out of focus; small, bright ones in focus. Concept confirmed by Blurry ("points are displaced in a circle depending on how far away they are from the focal plane"); the size/alpha form is the standard real-time adaptation. — Codrops / Blurry (URL in 13) `[recalled]` (concept verified)
76. **Blurry parameter set** — `cameraFocalDistance: 100`, `bokehStrength: 0.02`, `focalPowerFunction: 1` (0 = linear, 1 = non-linear falloff), `exposure: 0.009`, `distanceAttenuation`, `minimumLineSize` (screen-space thickness of in-focus lines), `useBokehTexture: true` with `pentagon2.png`. — Blurry (URLs in 13) `[verified]`
77. **Bokeh shape** — sample a small bokeh texture (pentagon/hexagon) in the fragment shader for out-of-focus sprites, cross-fade from the soft disc by CoC: `mix(disc, bokehTex, smoothstep(0.,1.,coc))`; costs a texture fetch ("a bit slower"). — Blurry (URLs in 13) `[verified]` (the cross-fade is recalled)
78. **Rim-lit bokeh without a texture** — `float ring = smoothstep(0.5,0.42,d) * mix(1.0, smoothstep(0.25,0.48,d), coc);` — brighter edge on defocused discs imitates a real lens. — general `[recalled]`
79. **Simple CoC curves** — `blur = smoothstep(minDistance, maxDistance, abs(z - focusZ));` (0 in focus, 1 fully out) or linear `blurRadius = abs(depth - targetDistance) * blurRate`. — Lettier, https://lettier.github.io/3d-game-shaders-for-beginners/depth-of-field.html ; Dave Pagurek, https://www.davepagurek.com/blog/depth-of-field/ `[verified]`
80. **Thin-lens CoC (physical)** — `c = |A · f · (d - s)| / (d · (s - f))` with aperture A, focal length f, focus distance s, object distance d — use only if you want real f-stop behaviour; 79 is enough for art direction. — general optics `[recalled]`
81. **Post-process DOF settings** — `new DepthOfFieldEffect(camera, { focusDistance: 3.0, focusRange: 2.0, bokehScale: 1.0, resolutionScale: 0.5 })`, world units; `effect.target = new Vector3()` gives autofocus on a point. — pmndrs, https://pmndrs.github.io/postprocessing/public/docs/class/src/effects/DepthOfFieldEffect.js~DepthOfFieldEffect.html `[verified]`
82. **Why post DOF fails on additive particles** — particles with `depthWrite:false` leave no depth, so a depth-based DOF pass blurs them by the background's depth; and MSAA "produces visual artifacts with depth-based effects". Use 75 for particles, post DOF only for opaque meshes and lines that write depth. — pmndrs wiki, https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing/wiki/Antialiasing `[recalled]` (MSAA caveat verified)
83. **Depth-aware gather rule** — a farther sample contributes if inside the *target* pixel's blur radius; a nearer sample contributes if inside the *sample's own* radius — prevents sharp foreground halos. — Dave Pagurek (URL in 79) `[verified]`
84. **Focus pull on scroll** — animate `uFocus` between sections (focus on the near rim of the sphere, then deep in the funnel throat); rack focus is a cheap "cinematic" beat. — general `[recalled]`
85. **Cheap DOF + bloom, off on mobile** — Bruno Simon's 2025 folio runs TSL `bloom` and `cheapDOF` in the render pipeline, driven by quality presets; mobile preset disables DOF/blur and lowers shadow resolution. — https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html ; https://deepwiki.com/brunosimon/folio-2025 `[verified]`
86. **Depth fade (fog for particles)** — `vAlpha *= smoothstep(uFar, uNear, -viewPosition.z);` plus colour shift toward background; exponential form `1.0 - exp(-density * dist)`. Beer's law `transmittance *= exp(-density * step)`. — Maxime Heckel (URL in 25) `[verified]` (Beer's law verified; the particle fade line is recalled)
87. **Near-camera fade** — `vAlpha *= smoothstep(0.2, 1.5, -viewPosition.z);` — removes giant sprites that pass through the camera (also the worst overdraw). — general `[recalled]`
88. **Frame-rate independent damping** — `x = lerp(x, target, 1.0 - exp(-lambda * dt));` equivalently `1 - pow(smoothing, dt)` with `lambda = -ln(smoothing)`; never `lerp(x, target, 0.1)` per frame (120 Hz screens then move twice as fast). `THREE.MathUtils.damp(x, y, lambda, dt)` implements it. — Rory Driscoll, https://www.rorydriscoll.com/2016/03/07/frame-rate-independent-damping-using-lerp/ `[verified]` (the three.js helper is recalled)

## 5. Post-processing

98. **Renderer flags for a composer pipeline** — `new WebGLRenderer({ powerPreference: "high-performance", antialias: false, stencil: false, depth: false })`. — pmndrs README (URL in 14) `[verified]`
99. **Merge effects** — one `EffectPass(camera, bloom, vignette, noise, toneMapping)` compiles them into a single fullscreen shader; fewer render operations than one pass per effect. — pmndrs README `[verified]`
100. **pmndrs Bloom defaults** — `luminanceThreshold: 1.0, luminanceSmoothing: 0.03, mipmapBlur: true, intensity: 1.0, radius: 0.85, levels: 8`, blend SCREEN. With an HDR buffer and threshold 1.0 only values above white bloom → selective bloom for free: make what should glow > 1.0. — https://pmndrs.github.io/postprocessing/public/docs/class/src/effects/BloomEffect.js~BloomEffect.html `[verified]`
101. **UnrealBloomPass reference values** — `new UnrealBloomPass(resolution, 1.5, 0.4, 0.85)` (strength, radius ∈ [0,1], threshold). For soft selective glow: strength 0.4–1.0, radius 0.5–1.2, threshold 0.1. — https://threejs.org/docs/pages/UnrealBloomPass.html ; https://waelyasmina.net/articles/unreal-bloom-selective-threejs-post-processing/ `[verified]`
102. **Threshold-free "physically based" bloom** — real lenses bloom everything; skip the threshold, blur the HDR image through a mip chain and `mix(scene, bloom, 0.04)`. Looks natural and never flickers at the threshold. — LearnOpenGL (Jimenez / Call of Duty method), https://learnopengl.com/Guest-Articles/2022/Phys.-Based-Bloom `[verified]`
103. **Mip-chain kernel** — downsample with a 13-tap filter (weights `0.125·5 + 0.03125·4 + 0.0625·4 = 1`), upsample with a 3×3 tent `[1 2 1; 2 4 2; 1 2 1]/16`, 5–6 mips; first downsample uses Karis average `1/(1+luma)` to stop fireflies pulsing (very relevant for tiny bright particles). — LearnOpenGL (URL in 102) `[verified]`
104. **Bloom before tone mapping** — bloom operates on HDR values; tone map afterwards. — LearnOpenGL (URL in 102); three.js forum (URL in 15) `[verified]`
105. **Selective bloom, classic recipe** — bloom layer `BLOOM_SCENE = 1`; before the bloom render swap non-bloom materials for `MeshBasicMaterial({color: 0x000000})`, render bloom composer (not to screen), restore, then final composer mixes `gl_FragColor = texture2D(baseTexture, vUv) + vec4(1.0) * texture2D(bloomTexture, vUv);`. `OutputPass` only on the final composer. — Wael Yasmina (URL in 101) `[verified]`
106. **Preferred order** — scene (HDR, linear) → DOF → bloom → chromatic aberration → tone mapping → vignette / grain (display-referred) → sRGB conversion → dither. Tone mapping "the last pass I add to my composer" among HDR effects. — three.js forum (URL in 15) `[recalled]` (tone-mapping-last and bloom-before are verified; the rest is convention)
107. **Chromatic aberration (transition-peaked)** — `float shift = dt * uRGBShift; r = texture2D(t, uv + vec2(shift,0.)).r; b = texture2D(t, uv - vec2(shift,0.)).b;` with `dt = parabola(progress, 2.)` — only present while shapes morph. — Codrops (URL in 37) `[verified]`
108. **Radial / velocity chromatic aberration** — offset along `normalize(uv-0.5) * length(uv-0.5) * k`; or cursor-driven: `influence = smoothstep(uRadius, 0., length(vUv-uMouse)) * uVelo;` sampling r/g/b at `-1.5, +0.5, +1.8 × offset`. — Codrops (URL in 37) `[verified]` (the radial form is recalled)
109. **Multi-sample dispersion** — loop `LOOP` samples, `slide = float(i)/float(LOOP)*0.1`, sample r/g/b with offsets scaled `1.0/2.0/3.0 × slide`, divide by `LOOP` — smooth spectral fringes instead of three hard ghosts. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/refraction-dispersion-and-other-shader-light-effects/ `[verified]`
110. **Film grain** — `color += (hash(uv*res + fract(uTime)*100.) - 0.5) * 0.04 * (1.0 - luma);` after tone mapping; stronger in shadows, animated each frame. Doubles as dither against banding. — Codrops grain-on-scroll tutorial (concept), https://tympanus.net/codrops/2024/07/18/how-to-create-distortion-and-grain-effects-on-scroll-with-shaders-in-three-js/ `[recalled]`
111. **Vignette** — `color *= smoothstep(0.85, 0.25, length(uv - 0.5));` — pulls the eye to the centre and hides low particle density at the edges. — general `[recalled]`
112. **Anti-aliasing with a composer** — canvas `antialias` has no effect once you render to targets. Use `new EffectComposer(renderer, { multisampling: 4 })` (WebGL2 MSAA, "high quality at low cost") unless depth-based effects are present, then use `SMAAEffect`. — pmndrs wiki (URL in 82) `[verified]` (the value 4 is recalled)
113. **Core three.js equivalent** — `new WebGLRenderTarget(w, h, { samples: renderer.getPixelRatio() === 1 ? 2 : 0, type: HalfFloatType })` passed to `EffectComposer`; add `SMAAPass` only when DPR is 1 and MSAA is unavailable; at DPR ≥ 2 skip AA. — Three.js Journey post-processing (paid part) `[recalled]`
114. **Composer resize** — on resize call `composer.setSize(w,h)` and `composer.setPixelRatio(min(dpr,2))`; bloom resolution follows. — Three.js Journey (paid part) `[recalled]`
115. **Composite rendering for scene transitions** — render scene A and B into render targets, mix in one fullscreen shader: `mix(toTex, fromTex, uTransition)`; wipe `step(uTransition, vUv.y)`; diagonal soft wipe `smoothstep(uTransition, uTransition+0.3, (vUv.x+vUv.y)/2.0)`. — Codrops, https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/23/composite-rendering-the-brilliance-behind-inspiring-webgl-transitions/ `[verified]`
116. **One composite shader instead of many passes** — fold blur, colour and distortion into the single composite shader; fullscreen quad in clip space with `renderOrder = -1`. — Codrops (URL in 115) `[verified]`
117. **Fullscreen overlay without matrices** — `PlaneGeometry(2,2)`, vertex `gl_Position = vec4(position, 1.0);`, fragment `vec4(0.,0.,0.,uAlpha)`, `transparent: true`. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/intro-and-loading-progress `[verified]`
118. **Ping-pong rule** — you cannot read a render target while writing to it; alternate two targets (what EffectComposer does internally). — Three.js Journey (URL in 26) `[verified]`
119. **Temporal trails / afterimage** — `trail = max(prev * decay - 0.025, current)`, `decay` 0.75–0.98, run at 0.25–0.5 resolution — light streaks behind fast particles during morphs. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/shading-motion/ `[verified]`
120. **Luma constant** — `dot(vec3(0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722), c)` — for thresholds, grain weighting, saturation. — Maxime Heckel, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/post-processing-as-a-creative-medium/ `[verified]`
121. **Progressive depixelation as a reveal** — `level = floor(progress * LEVELS); px = max(basePx / pow(2., level), 1.); uv = (px/res) * floor(uv / (px/res));` — intro reveal of the whole canvas from blocks to full resolution. — Maxime Heckel (URL in 120) `[verified]`

123. **Fresnel term** — `pow(1.0 - abs(dot(eye, normal)), power)` — rim glow for any solid/glass object placed among particles. — Maxime Heckel (URL in 109) `[verified]`

## 8. Performance

176. **DPR cap** — Codrops reference: desktop max 1.0, mobile max 1.5 (`Math.min(cap, devicePixelRatio)`). The common compromise is `min(dpr, 2)`; with additive particles and bloom, 1.5 is usually indistinguishable from 2. — https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/11/building-efficient-three-js-scenes-optimize-performance-while-maintaining-quality/ `[verified]` (`min(dpr,2)` is recalled)
177. **Adaptive quality** — monitor FPS (`PerformanceMonitor bounds [30, 500], flipflops 1`); on decline: DPR −20%, then disable post-processing, then remove objects. Vanilla equivalent: rolling average of frame time, step DPR by 0.25 with hysteresis. — Codrops (URL in 176) `[verified]` (the vanilla variant is recalled)
178. **Pause when hidden** — on `visibilitychange` stop the loop when `document.hidden`; also pause with an `IntersectionObserver` when the canvas is off-screen. — Codrops (URL in 176) `[verified]` (IntersectionObserver is recalled)
179. **Idle guard** — stop the rAF loop after 90 frames without input, resume on the next event — for any secondary canvas (cursor flowmap, hover effects). — Codrops (URL in 37) `[verified]`
180. **Measure properly** — stats.js (`showPanel(0)`), Spector.js for draw calls, `console.log(renderer.info)`; unlock Chrome's frame-rate cap to see real headroom; target 60 fps minimum on mid devices. — Three.js Journey, https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/performance-tips `[verified]`
181. **Overdraw is the real cost of additive particles** — the budget is the sum of sprite areas in pixels, not vertex count: 28k points × 20 px² is trivial, 28k × 400 px² is not. Clamp `gl_PointSize` (`min(size, uMaxSize)`), fade near-camera sprites (87), and shrink CoC on low tiers. — general `[recalled]`
182. **Half-resolution effects** — DOF `resolutionScale: 0.5` by default; bloom mips are already progressively smaller; motion/trail buffers at 0.25–0.5. — pmndrs DOF docs (URL in 81); Maxime Heckel (URL in 119) `[verified]`
183. **Mobile preset** — disable DOF and blur passes, lower shadow/RT resolution (Bruno Simon); reduce data: 4,096 vertices desktop vs 1,024 mobile (Lusion). For this site: ~10–14k particles, no post DOF, bloom at 5 levels, DPR 1.5. — Bruno Simon case study (URL in 85); Lusion (URL in 91) `[verified]` (the suggested preset is recalled)
184. **Animate in the vertex shader** — "we need to do that in the vertex shader in order to achieve good performance"; never rewrite 28k positions on the CPU per frame. — Three.js Journey (URL in 56) `[verified]`
185. **Disable frustum culling on morphing points** — `points.frustumCulled = false` (or set a large bounding sphere) because the CPU bounding volume belongs to shape 1 and the object will pop out when the camera moves. — general `[recalled]`
186. **Precision** — `precision highp float` in vertex shaders for positions/noise; `mediump` is acceptable in simple fragment shaders on mobile; avoid large `uTime` values (wrap time) to keep `sin`/hash stable in mediump. — general `[recalled]`
187. **Generate off the main thread** — geometry loading and particle generation in Web Workers; dirty-flag matrix updates ("only doing the calculation if something has truly changed"). In three.js: `matrixAutoUpdate = false` on static objects. — Active Theory, https://medium.com/active-theory/the-story-of-technology-built-at-active-theory-5d17ae0e3fb4 `[verified]` (three.js flag is recalled)
188. **Asset pipeline** — KTX2 (ETC1S/UASTC) textures, channel packing, Draco, `gltf-transform` scripts; `gltfjsx -S -T -t` cut assets by 90% in one Codrops build. — Immersive Garden, https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-immersive-gardens-new-website.html ; Bruno Simon (URL in 85); Codrops (URL in 176) `[verified]`
189. **Compact binary data** — 16-bit integers instead of 32-bit floats for baked animation/positions (Lusion: 983 KB desktop / 246 KB mobile); quantise the five shape buffers the same way if they are shipped rather than generated. — Lusion (URL in 91) `[verified]`
190. **Generate shapes procedurally** — sphere, funnel, helix, wave and galaxy are all closed-form; compute the five Float32Arrays at startup (a few ms for 28k × 5) and ship zero geometry bytes. — general `[recalled]`
191. **Loading order** — two-phase loading that brings the renderer up first (Bruno Simon folio); staged preloads: fonts immediately, hero on hover, first project during intro, rest deferred; build-time `image-dimensions.json` and inline LQIP. — deepwiki folio-2025 (URL in 85); Codrops (URL in 37) `[verified]`
192. **No framework state per frame** — at 120 Hz a React render costs about 8 ms; write to `element.style` / uniforms directly and bind the render function once. — Codrops (URL in 37) `[verified]`
193. **Physics / secondary systems at lower rate** — e.g. `timeStep = 1/30`; same idea for any CPU-side helper (neighbour links, label projection): update every 2nd–3rd frame. — Codrops (URL in 176) `[verified]`
194. **Dispose on navigation** — dispose geometries, materials, render targets and kill ScrollTriggers when leaving a page in an SPA. — Codrops (URL in 149); GSAP (URL in 146) `[verified]`
195. **WebGPU when available** — Bruno Simon's folio "automatically runs on WebGPU when available", WebGL fallback, TSL shaders shared by both. Worth it only if the particle system moves to compute; not needed for 28k attribute-morphed points. — Bruno Simon case study (URL in 85) `[verified]`
196. **GPU tiering** — classify devices at startup (e.g. `detect-gpu` tier 0–3) to pick a preset before the first frame instead of waiting for FPS to fall. — general `[recalled]`

## Top 25 upgrades for a scroll-morphing particle site

Ordered by visual impact per unit of effort (highest first). Numbers refer to items above.

1. **Noise-staggered morph** instead of a linear mix (32, 33) — 10 lines of GLSL, changes the whole character of the transitions.
2. **Parabola-peaked curl turbulence during transitions** (38, 64) — shapes stay crisp at rest, erupt between.
3. **Per-particle depth of field with energy-conserving alpha** (75, 87) — real depth, no post pass, fixes the "flat cloud" look.
4. **HDR half-float pipeline + tone mapping last (AgX or ACES)** (14, 15, 16, 17) — removes clipping to white, gives white-hot cores with coloured halos.
5. **Sprite quality: light-point / soft disc that reaches zero at the edge** (4, 6, 7) — every pixel of the site improves.
6. **Bloom on HDR: threshold 1.0 or threshold-free at ~0.04 mix, mip blur** (100, 102, 103) — clean, non-flickering glow; selective for free.
7. **Hold zones + eased progress per section** (40, 41) — each shape is readable while its text is on screen.
8. **Frame-rate independent damping everywhere** (88) — identical feel at 60 and 120 Hz.
9. **Lenis + GSAP + render in one ticker** (137, 139, 143) — no scroll/WebGL desync, no doubled smoothing.
10. **Size and brightness variety per particle** (2, 27, 30) — breaks the "uniform dots" look.
11. **Dither / grain after tone mapping** (24, 110) — kills banding on the dark background.
12. **Idle life at rest** (48, 71) — stateless curl + sin drift so nothing is ever frozen.
13. **Scroll-velocity layer** (156, 159, 203) — turbulence, bloom and chromatic aberration respond to how the user scrolls.
14. **Masked line reveals with SplitText `mask` + `autoSplit`** (160–163) — large typography on a par with the canvas.
15. **Mouse parallax on a camera group with tiny amplitude** (90, 91) — depth cue for almost no code.
16. **Cosine palette / colour by radius-noise-progress, one grade knob** (20, 21, 22) — a colour story per shape.
17. **Lines that share the morph shader; fat lines with a glow profile** (125, 129, 132) — DNA rungs and structure lines travel with the particles.
18. **Depth fade + dust layers** (86, 95) — atmosphere and scale.
19. **Chromatic aberration tied to the transition envelope** (107, 200) — lens "stress" only while morphing.
20. **Proximity snap to shapes** (142 or 145) — the site always settles on a composed frame.
21. **DPR cap + adaptive quality + tier presets** (176, 177, 183, 207) — holds 60 fps on laptops and phones.
22. **Overdraw control** (181, 87) — clamp point size and fade near sprites; the main mobile win.
23. **Intro sequence: counter, inset wipe, cloud assembles into the sphere** (168, 169, 206) — first five seconds decide the impression.
24. **Focus pull and camera spline between sections** (84, 93, 94) — cinematic beats once the basics above are in.
25. **Pause when hidden, idle guards, frustum-culling fix, dispose** (178, 179, 185, 194) — no visible gain, removes heat, battery drain and pop-out bugs.
